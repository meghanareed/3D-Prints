# 05 -- Print settings

The projects in `out/3mf` are Bambu Studio projects for a **Bambu Lab P2S with a
0.4 mm nozzle**. Open one and everything on this page is already set, including a
brim on each of the parts that needs one. Nothing below needs doing by hand.

This page is for everybody else. The STL plates in `out/plates` are geometry and
nothing else; these are the settings that go with them. None of it is exotic --
the only unusual entries are the brim list and *avoid crossing walls*, and both
are here because a plate came off the bed wrong without them.

It is generated by `mf3.py` from the same profile the projects carry, so it
cannot drift from them. Do not edit it by hand.

The brim is set in two places on purpose: `brim_type = outer_only` as the plate
default, and again on each of the parts below. Either one alone would do it if
everything loads; both, and the plate still has brims if the per-object settings
are ever dropped.

## The five that matter

| | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **An outer brim, 5 mm, on the 65 parts listed below** | Auto brim looks at a 6 mm^2 keystone, decides it is fine, and it comes off as spaghetti |
| 2 | **Avoid crossing walls** | The window interiors came out webbed with strings; every one was a travel move across the opening |
| 3 | **Bed 65 C first layer, 60 C after** | The wall faces and the base pan are big flat parts and they lift at 55 |
| 4 | **Do not re-orient anything** | Every part is exported already lying the way it should print. `orient.py` chose these; see the note at the bottom |
| 5 | **No supports** | Nothing in the kit needs them in its print orientation. If your slicer wants to add some, the part is the wrong way up |

The profile is Bambu's own `0.20mm Standard @BBL P2S` with the changes below. Anything not listed here
is stock and does not matter to this model -- match your own slicer's PLA
defaults and you will be fine.

## Filament

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Material | PLA |
| Nozzle | 220 C first layer, 220 C after |
| Bed (textured PEI) | 65 C first layer, 60 C after |
| Plate type | Textured PEI Plate |
| Flow ratio | 0.98 |

**The bed temperature is the one deliberate departure from stock PLA.** Bambu's
`Generic PLA @BBL P2S` runs the bed at 55 C throughout. The parts here that fail
are the big flat ones -- the wall faces are 240 x 100 mm and 2.5 mm thick, the
base pan is nearly the whole bed -- and at 55 C the corners lift. The projects
carry a `Bambu PLA Basic @BBL P2S - Large Flats` profile which is stock PLA with the bed at
65/60 C, and every
object on every plate is assigned to **filament slot 6**, which is
that profile. One slot for everything means no tool changes and no purge tower;
the small parts do not mind the warmer bed.

If your AMS is loaded differently, remap the slot in the Filament panel -- the
assignment is a slot number, not a colour. If you are on a printer without an AMS,
ignore the slot entirely and just raise the bed.

## Adhesion

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Plate default brim | outer_only |
| Brim width | 5 mm |
| Brim-object gap | 0.1 mm |
| Skirt loops | 0 |
| Per-object brim | `outer_only`, on the parts listed below |

Outer brim, not Auto, and not outer-and-inner. Auto is what failed. It gave no
brim to `19C_L_L_Wall_Plaque` (15 mm^2 on the bed) or `13As_L_L_Window_A_Sill` (49 mm^2 on the bed),
and both of those came off the plate mid-print. The smallest part on the list,
`29B_R_R_Keystone`, has 5 mm^2 -- about a grain of rice.

Inner brims are worse than useless here: they land inside the window openings,
where they are unreachable to pick out.

A part is on the list if any of four things is true of it in its print
orientation, measured off the mesh by `build.needs_brim`:

* its first layer is under **25 mm^2** -- too little grip, full stop;
* it is over **2x** as tall as its footprint is narrow -- it will be knocked over;
* it has over **50 mm^2** hanging down and more than 4x as much
  overhang as bed -- the slicer calls this a floating cantilever, and the peeling
  force of the unsupported material exceeds what the footprint can hold;
* or it is over **150 mm** across and either under **6 mm**
  tall or putting less than **25%** of its own footprint on the
  bed. Adhesion area is not the problem for these -- the wall face has 31,000 mm^2
  on the bed. What lifts is the perimeter: a wide sheet shrinks as it cools and
  has no height behind its edge to resist curling, and a wide skeleton is the same
  thing in strips.

## Supports

Off. `enable_support = 0`.

Every part is exported lying the way `orient.py` chose for it, and that choice
already accounts for overhangs -- it is what the choice is for. The one thing that
looks like it needs support and does not is the facade: parts such as
`11Ac_L_L2_Bay_Window_Corbel` and `19C_L_L_Wall_Plaque` sit pegs-up, so the
slicer sees small towers sticking into the air. Those are the P1 mounting pegs.
They are 2.5 x 2.0 x 3.5 mm, they print fine unsupported, and support material
around them would have to be picked out of the one feature on the part whose
dimensions have to be right.

## Orientation

**Do not let the slicer re-orient anything, and do not lay a part flat because it
looks unstable.** The orientation in the file is the answer to a search over the
24 axis-aligned placements of each part, scored on overhang area, footprint and
tippiness, with the facade parts pinned pegs-up so the peg faces are not printed
into a support interface. Re-orienting a part loses that and, on the facade, loses
the fit of the mount as well.

Arranging is fine -- move parts around the bed as you like. Rotating about Z is
fine. Rotating about X or Y is not.

## Not on a P2S

Nothing in the geometry is P2S-specific. The bed is 256 x 256 mm and every plate
is packed to fit it; a smaller bed means splitting the bigger plates, which the
STL plates in `out/plates` do not do for you -- load the individual STLs from
`out/stl` instead and pack them yourself.

On another Bambu machine, or in Orca: open the project and change the printer.
The per-object brims survive, because they are stored on the object rather than in
the printer profile.

In PrusaSlicer or SuperSlicer: the project will not open with its settings. Load
the STL plates, set the table above, and use a per-object modifier for the brim
list (right-click an object -> Add settings -> Skirt and brim).

In Cura: same, and note that Cura's *Combing mode: Not in Skin* is the nearest
thing to *avoid crossing walls*. Set brim per-object with a Per Model Setting.

## What this kit changes about the stock profile

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `reduce_crossing_wall` | `1` | **changed**. Avoid crossing walls -- the window interiors came out webbed with strings, and every one of them was a travel move straight across the opening |
| `brim_type` | `outer_only` | **changed**. The PLATE default, belt to the per-object braces below. Auto is what gave 19C and 13As no brim and let them come off the plate. Set here as well as on the objects because the object setting is invisible until you select the object, and because a plate that reads Auto in the Global tab is indistinguishable from a plate whose settings did not load at all |
| `brim_width` | `5` | pinned. 5 mm holds the 15 mm^2 plaques down and still peels off |
